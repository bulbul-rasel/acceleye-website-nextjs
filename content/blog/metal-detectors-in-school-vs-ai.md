School safety is an ongoing debate in the mid-2020s, with AI technologies now challenging metal detectors as the traditional solution.

## The legacy of metal detectors

- Metal detectors entered schools in the 1980s, mainly in urban areas, as part of a “get tough on crime” approach.
- Use peaked in the late 1990s, when about 10% of U.S. schools used them.
- As of 2017, New York City schools reported that 57% of weapons were found without scanning devices.
- One dangerous item was found for every 23,000 students scanned, and searches create long lines.
- A review of 15 years of research found no apparent effect on injuries, deaths or threats of violence on school grounds.

## Unintended consequences

- Studies link metal detectors to poorer student-teacher relationships and higher odds of students avoiding school.
- Schools with metal detectors tend to have more Black students and fewer white students, which may worsen existing inequities.
- Detectors can weaken students’ sense of safety and the overall school climate.

## The rise of AI in school security

- AI can detect a wider range of threats, including behavioral anomalies, not just metal objects.
- It can better tell harmless objects from potential weapons, reducing disruption.
- It analyzes security camera feeds continuously, flagging threats early.
- It can be added to existing video systems with minimal effort and cost.

## Comparing the two approaches

| | Metal detectors | AI |
|---|---|---|
| **Effectiveness** | Often fail to identify weapons, with failure rates reportedly as high as 95% in similar settings such as airports. | Potentially identifies a broader range of threats and analyzes patterns to predict incidents. |
| **Student experience** | Create long lines and instill fear. | Operates more discreetly, potentially reducing anxiety while maintaining vigilance. |
| **Cost & implementation** | Significant upfront costs and ongoing staffing. | Uses existing video systems, potentially reducing long-term staffing, and scales. |

## The way forward

- Metal detectors are a visible response, but their effectiveness is increasingly questioned.
- AI-powered solutions may be more comprehensive and less intrusive.
- Technology alone cannot solve every school safety issue. As University of Wisconsin professor Ben Fisher puts it: “Make sure every student has an adult in the building who they know cares about them.”
- A balanced approach that pairs technology with community-building is the most effective.
